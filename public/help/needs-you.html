HelpEveryday work

Handle something that needs you

Questions, approvals and requests from your Ducks wait on one page. Here is how to answer each kind.

13 stepsChecked against the app on 7 October 2026

Step12345678910111213
  1. 1

    Step 1: Find what’s waiting

    Click Needs you in the sidebar.

    Waiting for you holds questions, approvals, blocked connections, screen requests, proposals, tickets that are your turn, and unfinished work. To read when you like holds updates that hold nobody up. Click a row to open it.

    Good to know. The number in the sidebar counts both lists, so not everything is urgent. Screen requests with a countdown come first; the rest is newest first.

  2. 2

    Step 2: Answer a question

    Open the question, click Reply, and answer in the chat.

    Reply opens the chat, and the thread, the question came from. Give the missing choice, like “Start with the Netherlands, small businesses.” If it shows Open the ticket, answer on the ticket instead.

    Watch out. Take off my list only hides the message. It doesn’t answer anything.

  3. 3

    Step 3: Fill in a form for a Duck

    Fill in the fields and click Send to [Duck].

    TameDuck types the values into the website for the Duck; they are not kept in chat. For a login or a code only you can enter, click Do it on [Duck]’s screen instead, finish it, and hand the screen back.

    Watch out. When the countdown ends, the Duck carries on without you, and what you typed is not sent. Click I’m back, try again if you see it, or ask the Duck again.

    Good to know. Tell [Duck] to stop stops that run. See Let a Duck use a computer for the screen controls.

  4. 4

    Step 4: Say no to a screen request

    Click Say no, pick a quick reason or write your own, and click Send to [Duck].

    The Duck doesn’t get your screen and carries on another way. Your reason helps it choose, and it won’t ask for the screen again in that task. If it can’t finish without you, it says what’s left. Clicking Back closes the form without answering.

    Watch out. Say no and Tell [Duck] to stop are different. Say no only refuses the screen, and the Duck keeps working. Stop ends the whole task.

    Good to know. Afterwards the chat shows You said no, with your reason.

  5. 5

    Step 5: Approve a tool action

    Click See what it sends, check every value, then click Approve.

    Check where it goes, who gets it, amounts and content. Approving runs exactly that action.

    • Ask for changes lets you write a note, then send it to the Duck. Back returns without sending.
    • Decline says no.

    Watch out. Typing “yes” in the chat does not approve. Use the buttons on the card.

    Good to know. Once you decide, the item leaves this page and the chat records it. If the service fails, read the result before you assume it worked.

  6. 6

    Step 6: Review a new task board

    Click See all settings, read everything, then click Approve.

    Chief Duck can propose a new board, or changes to one. Check its steps, who works on each, the instructions, who approves, and whether work starts or moves on by itself. Ask for changes sends a note back; Decline says no.

    Watch out. Changing a step’s Duck, instructions or approvers starts a new attempt for the tickets in that step.

    Good to know. Leave Always let Chief change this board off to check every change yourself. The card says how long the proposal stays open. See Track work on the task board.

  7. 7

    Step 7: Check a new skill

    Click See all settings to read the instructions and which Ducks get it, then click Approve.

    Approving lets those Ducks use the skill the next time they work. It doesn’t start a task. A skill saved as turned off can’t be used until it is turned on.

    Watch out. Expired or replaced? Ask Chief Duck for a new proposal instead of approving the old one.

    Good to know. Deciding needs permission to manage skills, Ducks and approvals. If the card says you can’t decide, ask your company owner.

  8. 8

    Step 8: Approve work that repeats

    Check how often it runs and where results go, then click Set it up, or No, don’t set it up.

    The card shows the first run, the time zone, where results appear and about how many runs a month. More runs means more AI use. Afterwards, Open scheduled tasks shows the schedule. Asked to change one? The card lists only what would be different, as it is now and as it would be: Change it applies it from the next run, Keep it as it is leaves it as it was.

    Watch out. Asked to remove a schedule? Stop it deletes the schedule and its list of runs; messages and tickets stay. Keep it leaves it running. A paused schedule stays paused after you fix the AI connection: open it and turn it back on.

    Good to know. Only tick Also let [Duck] schedule work without asking if it may start, change and stop regular work by itself. You can turn that off in Settings → Ducks.

  9. 9

    Step 9: Fix a connection or give access

    Click Allow to let the Duck use that service, or Reconnect to fix a broken sign-in or key.

    Allow gives the Duck access to the whole service, not just one action. Not now dismisses the request. Reconnect opens Settings → Connections, and Connect an AI opens Settings → AI connection.

    Good to know. Some fixes need an owner or admin. The card says when.

  10. 10

    Step 10: Do your step on a ticket

    Click Open the ticket and do what its step asks.

    Send back to … returns work to an earlier step, with a note. Post note only adds a comment and moves nothing.

    • Your step: do the work, write a handover in Your note, then click Finish your step. A review step offers Approve instead.
    • Stuck or Changes requested: fix the cause, add guidance in Your note, then click Start a new attempt.
    • Ready to move on: check the result, then click Move to … or Approve, move to ….

    Good to know. Replying in another chat, or taking the message off your list, doesn’t move the ticket. See Track work on the task board.

  11. 11

    Step 11: Pick up unfinished work

    Click Open the chat or Open the ticket to see what got done and what’s left.

    The card says why the work stopped and how many automatic retries were used. Fix what is missing, check what was already done, then click Continue if you see it.

    Good to know. Waiting to resume or Resuming soon means a retry is on its way. Stop automatic resume puts retries on hold; the saved work stays.

  12. 12

    Step 12: Read updates and clear your list

    Open an update, then click Take off my list when you’re done with it.

    Click Reply to talk about it in its chat. Taking it off your list leaves the message in its chat. It doesn’t delete anything, finish work or stop a Duck.

    Good to know. Nothing waiting on you means your own list is empty, not that all company work is done.

  13. 13

    Step 13: If something goes wrong

    Read the message on the card first. It says what happened.

    • The server could not be reached: your answer wasn’t confirmed. Check your connection and try again. A note you wrote is kept.
    • Expired, replaced or already answered: open the newest version and act on that.
    • The countdown ended: your form answer was not sent. Click I’m back, try again, or ask the Duck again.
    • No buttons to decide: you don’t have that permission. Ask your company owner, on Team.
    • Still there after you acted: wait for it to finish, then check the chat or ticket.

    Good to know. An item is done when its answer, decision or work is accepted, not when you open or hide it.

That’s it.
